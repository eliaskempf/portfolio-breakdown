    Prices and performance

    Valuation and freshness

    Reporting value = quantity × quote price × reporting currency per unit of quote currency

    Provider prices use the latest available unadjusted daily close; the current bar can still change. They are not guaranteed real-time execution quotes. Quote currency comes from provider metadata, not ticker suffixes. Recognized pence/cents units are converted to major currency before FX. A saved manual price overrides provider pricing until cleared. Buy-in is never substituted for market price.

    Missing ticker, currency, FX or quote leaves a position unvalued, not worth zero. A zero-quantity position has zero current value without requiring a quote. View price details for separate quote/FX timestamps, ages and reasons.

    Quotes and FX refresh in the background using private caches. Normal attempts are limited to once per 15 minutes per key, including failures. Refresh prices bypasses that interval. Failed refreshes can retain the last successful quote as cached fallback. Old data is not newly observed data; inspect dates across market closures. Crypto quotes older than 24 hours have a continuous-market note.

    Gains and returns

    Choose EUR, USD or GBP during manual setup or in Portfolio settings → Portfolio currency. Existing portfolios default to EUR. Changing this setting recalculates values and gains from original records; it does not change purchase amounts or currencies. Review affected positions before applying. Monetary planning inputs and previous plans are cleared when currency changes.

    Allocation and Performance All share total known gain: recorded realized and unrealized gains plus recorded distributions and interest, less recorded costs once. They use the same current valuation inputs, currency and scope, including retained closed positions. Quote and FX timestamps remain visible in price details; current inputs do not fill missing historical observations. Category comparison uses the same monetary calculation. ETF look-through does not redefine costs.

    The Overview percentage is cumulative time-weighted return (TWR) over the supported recorded interval. It neutralizes external contributions and withdrawals using daily valuations and the daily cash-flow convention described below. It is not gain divided by remaining cost or net capital. Annualized return is separately money-weighted (XIRR), reflecting contribution amounts and timing. Neither percentage is inferred from a partial monetary subtotal. Position details retain separately labelled unrealized gain and cost-based return.

    Partial means supported known components are shown, with missing facts in metric help. Summary-only holdings contribute their known unrealized gain; snapshot histories carry known opening unrealized gain but cannot establish earlier realized gains or income. Unreviewed records can support a recorded subtotal, never complete returns. Unknown amounts, unsupported actions and missing FX are not zero.

    Cash balances without a recorded cost/conversion basis show N/A — cash balance for purchase-based performance. They remain in portfolio value and allocation, but do not produce missing-buy-in warnings or reduce coverage of eligible positions. Balance increases from deposits or futures settlements are not inferred as cash investment gains. Interest is separate. Foreign cash still changes reporting value with current FX; calculating its FX gain needs a recorded opening or conversion basis. Explicit cash costs, including incomplete conversions, retain the existing cost-based calculation and coverage checks.

    Each purchase, including fees, is converted separately. A supplied converted cost takes precedence over a supplied rate, which takes precedence over historical market FX. Historical lookup uses the purchase date or the most recent available observation within the preceding seven days. It never uses a future observation. Market rates can differ from actual broker settlement rates. Same-currency costs need no purchase date or conversion.

    A position with incomplete costs has no complete cost-based gain, while its available current value still counts toward portfolio value and allocation. Transaction CSV can retain supplied acquisition-cost and monetary-valuation estimates, with their sources. Affected gains show an approximate status. Applying transaction history does not certify estimated amounts as exact; the label remains while the calculation depends on those estimates. Costed units can contribute a partial unrealized gain while other units retain unknown cost; the unknown units are never treated as free. Independently supported recorded income or realized gains can still contribute to an explicitly partial monetary subtotal. Complete, Partial and Unavailable describe coverage. A partial return is not the return of the entire category. Zero cost permits an absolute gain but no cost-based percentage. TWR/XIRR have their own flow and valuation requirements.

    For old costs without dates, supply a converted total or exchange rate, or explicitly confirm a latest FX estimate. Portfolio settings lets you select affected positions and review their rates before confirming. Estimates may hide currency gains or losses, remain labelled, and keep their confirmed rates across refreshes and restarts. An undated conversion or estimate applies only to its saved target currency; switching again may create new coverage gaps.

    Separately labelled ordinary-holding unrealized figures exclude dividends and realized gains. Purchase fees affect results only when included in recorded cost. Reconcile remaining-position costs after sales or corporate actions. Expanded ETF constituents have unavailable performance: current weights cannot reconstruct their historical costs. Whole-fund performance remains usable in instruments mode. Labels follow the same path splits as allocation.

    Futures investment details retain a separate recorded-performance basis: cumulative net P&L across open and closed contracts, including fees and manually entered taxes, divided by fixed initial allocated capital. Settlement does not erase gains. Allocation changes affect capital value, not profit. Current reporting FX converts native amounts; historical FX performance of contributed capital is not inferred. See Investment capital and contract history.

    Market-price history

    In position details, History → Price provides 1M, 6M, 1Y, 5Y and Max views of closing prices in the portfolio reporting currency, without dividend reinvestment. When the instrument's quote currency differs, the currency selector beside the period offers both currency codes (for example, EUR and USD). This is instrument history, not personal return history. It does not know your purchase dates or cash flows.

    History loads in the background and caches for one hour. A failed request waits 60 seconds before an automatic retry; Retry history bypasses the wait. Manual/unsupported instruments can have no chart. Normal demo mode uses public history; explicit offline demo history is invented. The separate risk calculation uses adjusted prices and historical FX.

    Personal history

    CSV, PDF and manually entered transactions use the same history and controls. Source details are available with each transaction; they do not change its calculations or editability. Exchanges and transfers are single economic events whose connected holdings are reviewed together.

    Explicitly supplied estimates can support daily TWR and annualized XIRR. Affected results retain the estimate qualification in gain/TWR and annualized-return tooltips, without separate estimate captions. Missing transaction details and market observations remain separate limitations. An estimated acquisition cost does not by itself make a return estimated when that return uses independently known cash flows and market values. Unknown amounts stay unknown; a matching ending quantity does not certify complete history. The former separate daily-Dietz diagnostic is retired. Existing diagnostic settings are preserved but no longer affect the application.

    The History → Holdings position view and Overview → Performance show personal history separately from market-price charts and current unrealized gains. History is optional; charts stay hidden until at least two supported values exist. Positions open on History, with Holdings selected when useful transaction history exists and Price otherwise. Positions without recorded history evidence show the price chart directly, without the selector. The Holdings / Price selector remembers your explicit choice for each position during the session. Without applied history, Holdings offers a route to add and review transactions. The other position tabs stay in the same order. Overview remembers your selected view for each portfolio, including after a browser refresh or app restart. With no saved choice it opens Performance when useful history exists, otherwise Allocation. Allocation's percentage gain uses full-history TWR; when unavailable, its tooltip explains the missing evidence. Position details share one row above the chart selector: Position value with personal gain, Invested capital, and Annualized return. Switching chart type, range or hover does not change these headlines. Dated figures stay in the chart tooltip. Details contains return on cost and unrealized gain. Without applied history, capital is the recorded acquisition cost, gain is unrealized only, and annualized return is unavailable. Overview keeps its own history summary above the chart. The category comparison stays visible below history, without repeating the category or position tables. Choosing another view retains its usual current-portfolio summary. Use 1M, 3M, 6M, YTD, 1Y, 3Y, 5Y or All to look back from today. The chart axis ends today; period gain uses the first and latest supported valuations in the selected window. Annualized return uses the full recorded interval. Verified closed-market endpoints can use the last eligible closing quote, retaining its actual as-of date. Stuttgart listings are recognized from the provider's exchange and timezone metadata and use the Stuttgart calendar, including its published trading holidays. A metadata refresh updates previously downloaded evidence without inventing weekend quotes. A missing price on a scheduled trading day still blocks the affected valuation. The latest valuation date remains visible. Lines connect neighboring known values across missing observations; this does not supply prices for calculation purposes.

    History follows the main Category selector: Portfolio includes the full portfolio; a category includes its current source-position membership, including retained closed positions. This does not reconstruct past category assignments. Removed closed positions with no current assignment remain in Unassigned and the portfolio total, including the category comparison. Accounts include cash, debt, pending claims and actual futures. An account whose components span categories needs dated allocations before category history can be shown; it is never silently counted in full or omitted. Allocated futures strategy capital is also unavailable at category level without that support. Unadopted legacy liabilities and simulations cannot silently disappear from a whole-portfolio result.

    All means total known gain, not necessarily the full ownership period. Missing opening prices never silently substitute a shorter interval. Complete histories start empty before the first purchase, include that payment and fees, and retain gains after liquidation. A confirmed empty start needs no positive snapshot.

    The solid Value line is the selected holding or portfolio's market value. The thinner dashed Invested capital line starts with known opening acquisition cost for securities, and recorded opening equity for accounts, then adds external contributions less withdrawals. Value minus capital equals cumulative known gain at the same date. After disposals this is not remaining acquisition cost and can be zero or negative. Unsupported capital remains unavailable. Changing the chart period preserves the basis. Nothing before an opening snapshot is represented as zero value or gain.

    For example, an invented snapshot with cost 8,000 and market value 10,000 starts with capital 8,000 and known gain 2,000. Later value 11,000 means known gain 3,000, but post-snapshot return is 10% before annualization. The earlier gain has unknown timing and does not enter returns. Backfill replaces this bridge rather than adding reconstructed gains to it a second time. If cost is unknown, measured post-snapshot gain may be shown as partial while capital stays unavailable. Move anywhere across the plot to inspect a date and formatted values together. The vertical guide snaps to observations; it does not invent interpolated prices. If series have different observation dates, the tooltip shows each differing date beside its value. The value and invested capital boxes follow the hovered observations and show their dates. Click the gain badge to toggle between an amount and cumulative TWR. The metric name and period appear only in a concise tooltip. Gain beside the value shows the selected-period gain at rest and cumulative recorded gain to the hovered date during inspection. Ranges covering the entire holding period use total known gain, just like All. Leaving the chart restores the latest value and selected-period gain. Annualized return stays fixed across both period selection and hover; its tooltip gives the calculation dates. Missing opening data or incomplete full-history coverage withholds this return rather than substituting a shorter interval. A quiet note below the chart indicates missing valuations; no values are interpolated for the boxes or returns. A missing daily value need not prevent endpoint-based returns. Returns for both All and a selected period remain unavailable if a later recorded event lacks an ending valuation. The timeline includes linked-security cash movements, incoming transfers and separately dated fees. Those fee dates also count when checking for known negative equity, even if the balance later recovers.

    Correct market data provides focused forms for missing or incorrect dated prices and exchange rates. Known dates, holdings and currencies are preselected; only the selected holdings' observations are shown. Advanced corrections retains whole-selection valuations, exact cash-flow boundary values and cash adjustment classification. Available prices and FX load automatically; Retry prices is shown after an unsuccessful refresh. Downloads include accepted exact-contract settlement marks. It does not create trades, distributions or splits. Downloaded closes are converted back to the units traded on each date using the provider's split evidence, without dividend reinvestment or automatic price repair. Manual observations take precedence. For prices, optional Quote provenance contains Source, Quote listing, Exchange calendar and Exchange timezone. Corrections initially retain the saved quote's details; missing dates can receive explicitly supplied details. These entries are your evidence, not automatic verification. The historical quote listing does not change the holding's current listing. Saving preserves the original observation and records a manual correction, so later downloads do not overwrite it. Saved market data shows actual source and listing/calendar/timezone details; manual source descriptions are marked manual:.

    Prices must cover required valuation boundaries; only verified closed sessions can use the preceding eligible close. Exact-contract futures marks use the supported Eurex session calendar too, so weekend funding can use the preceding session's settlement without inventing a weekend mark. Missing trading-day marks and unsupported calendars remain gaps. For Saturday/Sunday valuations, an absent closing FX rate uses the immediately preceding Friday's closing rate, or the prior scheduled close when Friday itself is a documented FX holiday. Thus a Monday contribution can use Friday's supported closing price and FX for its opening valuation, without inventing Sunday observations. The same rule covers explicitly documented FX closing-session holidays for EUR, USD, GBP, CHF, JPY, CAD, AUD and NZD pairs, including New Year's Day 2025. The historical date list uses OANDA's holiday schedule and LEAN's Forex-oanda calendar. Evening holiday reopenings start the next closing session. Other bank/equity holidays and undocumented future holidays do not establish an FX closure; maintainers must verify additional dates before adding them. Actual dated closing FX takes precedence. Missing scheduled closes are not filled from older or later rates. Quote and FX as-of dates remain visible. Transaction payments, fees and acquisition-cost conversions still require their own dated conversion evidence. Gaps are not interpolated or filled with current quotes. Missing data can be entered manually. Corrections retain original observations.

    Applied history is the authoritative ledger for the resulting holdings and accounts. Apply history accepts the balances shown in reconciliation; there is no separate completeness confirmation. Gains and returns remain available as new prices arrive, provided their required inputs are supported. A newer valuation date does not invalidate the applied ledger.

    Replacement balances interrupt an interval, and unresolved cash adjustments must be classified as external flows, income or expenses, or retained as boundaries. An edit to remaining acquisition cost alone does not interrupt period gain, TWR or XIRR: those use market values and cash flows. It does prevent carrying the old cost basis through the edit as complete lifetime capital or gain; independently supported gain components remain available. Unknown amounts, missing market data and unsupported earlier periods remain explicit limitations. Applying records does not prove that unrecorded real-world activity never occurred; calculations describe the records supplied. After a dated replacement opening, later periods can use that opening even if an older checkpoint lacked an observation date. Returns across the replacement remain unsupported.

    Account cash openings precede separately recorded same-day account trades, fees and external funding; these events must not disappear into the first closing valuation. Security starting balances remain end-of-day snapshots, and derived security payments already included in an account opening are not posted again. An explicitly newly funded account instead starts at zero before its first-day transactions, including linked securities purchases and fees. A confirmed empty security opening dated the preceding day is consistent with that declaration. If an internal transfer or asset-paid fee on the selected start date crosses a new account's opening boundary and an existing account or holding's closing snapshot, period gain and returns remain unavailable. Choose a start date before or after that day; the supported ending value remains available. Same-day flow order must be explicit for exact TWR.

    Metric Required evidence
    Historical value Dated holdings, cash, debt, claims, prices and FX, or an explicitly supplied total valuation
    Invested capital Known opening cost/account equity plus supported classified net external flows
    Total known gain Ending value minus capital, including known opening gains; partial components stay labelled
    Period gain Ending value minus opening market value minus net external contributions
    Annualized money-weighted return Endpoint values and complete dated external flows with a numerically verified unique XIRR
    Time-weighted return Supported daily flow boundaries and complete dated external flows; exact event valuations when explicitly requesting the exact method

    Purchases paid from included cash, transfers between included accounts, borrowing with its matching debt, and futures settlement are internal movements. Crossing the selected scope changes that treatment. Dividends and interest are income; withdrawals are separate flows. Fees reduce results once. Remaining acquisition cost and cumulative purchases are not net contributions. Futures notional and allocated principal are not extra wealth; settled profit already resides in cash.

    Money-weighted returns use actual elapsed days divided by 365 and can work without daily valuations. The annualized-return tooltip identifies short recorded intervals. Same-day periods, known negative-equity intervals and unverified solutions have no annualized return. Negative equity is checked at recorded event-day closes and valuation dates, including values reconstructed from prices, FX, cash and debt. Negative cash covered by other assets, and a negative invested-capital line after withdrawals, do not themselves make portfolio equity negative. Missing valuations remain unknown; intraday losses without recorded valuation evidence are not inferred. Empty inception and terminal liquidation are supported when dated flows establish a unique result. TWR can link explicitly valued funded intervals separated by an empty period; money appearing without funding is invalid. Mixed-sign cash flows are eligible only when the solver establishes a unique, numerically verified XIRR. Multiple roots, no solution and uncertain numerical conditioning are different from missing transactions. Supported long schedules use a verified uniqueness certificate. An XIRR calculation limit means the bounded solver could not verify the full schedule within its work limit; unusually complex schedules can still remain unavailable. The app never shortens the period, guesses a root or substitutes return on cost. Conventional schedules retain a separate fast path.

    Ordinary TWR uses a daily convention: incoming capital is available at the start of the day and outgoing capital leaves at its end. Chain daily factors (ending equity + withdrawals) / (beginning equity + contributions) and subtract one. This convention does not reconstruct intraday prices. Flow-free intervals can telescope without inventing intermediate observations; required flow boundaries must still be supported. Negative equity remains unsupported.

    Same-date charges belong to their economic transaction: purchase fees increase contributions, while sale/distribution charges reduce proceeds. Separately dated charges keep their dates. Recorded fees and taxes reduce returns once. Included cash and securities trade internally; external fee funding enters once. Scope membership determines which payments cross the boundary. Embedded fund expenses are already in market prices and are not deducted again.

    Exact event TWR remains an explicit calculation option. It requires genuine ordering and supported before/after event valuations, and includes the event's fees and income. Execution prices may support standalone security boundaries, but do not value other assets in a portfolio. Neither method changes saved trade dates. Historical value points and monetary gains can remain useful when returns cannot be calculated. The affected metric’s help explains its own missing requirements; other metrics do not inherit that restriction.

    Original acquisition costs remain separate from opening market value, so gains before the tracking boundary are not attributed to the tracked period. FIFO is analytical cost accounting, not a tax report. Complex unsupported corporate actions must remain unresolved until sufficient records or a new opening boundary exist.

    Position Details separates recorded realized gain, net income, other expenses, purchase payments and net sale proceeds. Remaining acquisition cost belongs to the unsold FIFO lots; cumulative purchase payments include all recorded purchases and their charges. Sale proceeds include returned principal, whereas realized gain deducts the sold lots' costs. Buy fees enter lot cost, sell and distribution charges reduce their proceeds, and other expenses are deducted separately. These components reconcile to total gain for an ordinary complete trade history. Transfers preserve lot costs but cross performance scopes at market value, so their acquisition-cost breakdown and scope gain have different boundaries.

    Imported swaps and rewards distinguish recorded asset units from supplied monetary valuations. A swap's analytical proceeds and incoming basis do not imply a cash payment. In-kind rewards add income and basis when their value is supplied, without an external contribution. Own-custody transfers preserve original lots; staking and unstaking do not recognize the same reward twice.

    Asset fees remove units once and expense FIFO carrying cost under the import's versioned portfolio policy. That expense is not an inferred market sale. Internal transfers and swaps cancel before valuing whole-portfolio flows. A selected subset may still need a transfer-date price or swap valuation. Thus portfolio gain can be supported while a particular lot cost, gain component or subset return remains unknown. See transaction import for evidence and review.
