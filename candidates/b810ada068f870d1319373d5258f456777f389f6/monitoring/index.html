    Monitoring and alerts

    Monitoring explores indicators without adding an asset to your portfolio. On a fresh visit, Browse opens MSCI World in EUR with a 200-session SMA and an empty trigger threshold. This Untracked preview shows prices and SMA only, without rule bands or condition status, and does not enable alerts. Existing saved rules continue monitoring independently. Enter a threshold to preview its bands and condition; Track rule becomes available once the required inputs and market history are present. Clearing the threshold returns to the plain chart.

    Under Browse, Market indices offers a fixed set of broad benchmarks: MSCI World, MSCI Emerging Markets and MSCI ACWI, each with published net-return levels in EUR, USD or GBP. Portfolio assets separately lists the unique tickers of currently held assets, excluding cash, derivatives and zero holdings. Provider ticker accepts an exact Yahoo Finance symbol for other instruments. Compare completed closes with an SMA and your trigger/approaching boundaries, then name the rule and choose Track rule in the save row above the chart. Tracked rules provides details, editing, pause/resume and deletion. Position details offer Track this asset; account and collateral views offer Create account alert.

    For an offline introduction, choose ? → Take the tour and use the chapter shortcuts to reach Monitoring. Its trend, saved rule, triggered alert and acknowledgment use a fresh temporary synthetic workspace. Your existing rules and acknowledgments are preserved.

    Moving-average rules

    The 200-session preset uses 200 completed trading observations, not 200 calendar days. The 10-month preset uses ten consecutive completed month-end closes, not all daily observations during ten months. Missing months are not filled. A newly completed close enters the average. Months finalize when the next local calendar month begins; a provider bar still developing during the trading session does not count as a completed close.

    Rules compare 100 × (price / SMA − 1) with an inclusive threshold. For example, a below rule with a −2% trigger fires at or below 98% of its SMA. A one-percentage- point approaching band begins at −1%. Above rules reverse the comparison.

    Choose completed daily close, completed month-end close, or a timestamped intraday observation. Intraday mode normally uses the last completed SMA. Include developing period, under Timing & data freshness, instead combines the current observation with the preceding N−1 completed periods. This changes the reference during the period and is explicitly labeled.

    Closing-price history is the default for stocks and ETFs. Dividend-adjusted history is a separate selection, rebased to current quote units for comparison. Provider split adjustments remain part of the chosen history. An index retains its named currency and price/net/gross-return convention. Rules retain their native series currency when the portfolio reporting currency changes.

    Direct MSCI references provide daily net-return levels, not intraday quotes. Choose an alternative instrument explicitly if you want intraday tracking; the app never silently substitutes an ETF. Regular-session provider quotes may be delayed. Freshness limits are editable: defaults are 30 minutes for intraday rules and seven days for close/account rules, entered in minutes, hours or days. Intraday rules can therefore show stale outside trading hours. Missing metadata, insufficient history, stale inputs or failed requests withhold a new signal and any all-clear.

    Yahoo can include entirely empty rows in historical downloads. Rows without any OHLC prices or trading volume are omitted, without filling prices; the SMA uses available closing observations. Partial bars with a missing close, or missing adjusted prices, remain data errors. Data details shows omitted-row counts, the source, its available history and retrieval time.

    Historical browsing

    Chart period offers one, three, five or ten years, all available history, dotcom and GFC periods, or custom start and end dates. Indicators are calculated using the full history before the chart is cropped, preserving their warm-up. Historical-period metrics describe the final close in that period and, when a threshold is configured, are labeled Historical condition. With a blank threshold, no condition is shown. Browsing the past neither creates alerts nor changes a saved rule's live evaluation period; it is not a strategy backtest.

    The MSCI source is requested from 2000 onward; actual availability varies by index. An ETF or other instrument cannot show prices from before its own available history. Missing initial SMA values are left blank until sufficient earlier observations exist. The app never silently substitutes an older proxy instrument.

    Account rules

    LTV divides recorded account loans plus negative cash obligations by the current gross value of your explicitly selected backing securities and positive cash balances. Cash uses the canonical account balance, so adopted cash is not counted twice. Linked securities remain ordinary holdings. No haircuts apply to this gross LTV measure. Pending transfers and futures notional are excluded. Choose your own maximum and warning LTV ratios; the app does not infer a broker's liquidation threshold.

    Maintenance headroom uses the existing pledged collateral after haircuts and entered maintenance requirements. Its percentage is 100 × (eligible collateral − maintenance requirement) / maintenance requirement. Choose trigger and warning headroom thresholds. The details show monetary headroom and the date/source of the entered requirement.

    Rules fix their calculation currency and show included inputs. Missing valuation or FX withholds the result. Positive debt with zero backing triggers an explicit undefined-ratio alert; zero debt with zero backing and zero maintenance requirements make their respective percentage metrics inapplicable. These are user-defined limits and declared coverage, not broker-specific liquidation models.

    Delivery and history

    Monitoring runs while an app session is open, checking every five minutes with a one-minute heartbeat. Last rule check and Check all rules now appear only in Tracked rules and refer to all enabled saved rules. A completed pass can include stale or unavailable results; check each rule's Data column. Refresh market data in Browse refreshes the selected instrument's prices and history without checking saved rules. Closing the app, suspending the computer or losing the session interrupts monitoring. This release does not install an operating-system background service or send external messages.

    Approaching conditions show a banner inside Monitoring by default. Triggered conditions also open an acknowledgment dialog on any tab, including Browse and inline editors. An already open modal defers the alert until it closes. A rule can instead use banners only or the notification center only. Notifications explain the affected rule and offer View rule. Existing data-quality notices have specific review actions.

    Reading and acknowledging are separate. Opening Notifications marks entries read; Acknowledge alert in the pop-up or Acknowledge in Notifications silences that alert and hides its banner. The current condition remains visible in Tracked rules. Full recovery to normal rearms the rule. Escalation from approaching to triggered produces a new alert, but unchanged checks and threshold jitter within one warning episode do not repeat pop-ups. Data failures preserve the last valid condition with an explicit stale label rather than treating missing data as normal.

    History retains observations and alert episodes from creation onward. Available missed completed-close events can be recovered on reopening and are labeled with the original observation date and later recording time. Intraday and account history is never reconstructed across offline gaps. Charts show source history; recorded observations show what monitoring actually evaluated. Provider revisions can change a historical chart without rewriting the observation log.

    Editing or pausing/resuming creates a new rule revision and evaluation baseline. Deleting a rule preserves its recorded history. Data lives in private monitoring.json and is included in workspace backups. Rule edits retain document backups; frequent observation writes use atomic replacement without making a full backup copy each time. Market downloads stay in private caches.
