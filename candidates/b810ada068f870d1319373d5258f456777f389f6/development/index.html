    Source development

    This page is for contributors working in a source checkout. Installed-app users can use their shortcut and the in-app controls; Python and uv are not needed. Start with source installation before running these commands.

    Interface design

    The main interface prioritizes quantitative results, exploration and actions. Labels, grouping and consistent controls should make the view understandable without introductory paragraphs or repeated explanations.

    • Keep visible prose minimal. Prefer concise labels and short, contextual status text. Put definitions in tooltips or help popovers, and methodology, assumptions, provenance and extended explanations in expandable details or the user guide. Treat prose as a removal signal during UI review: ordinary screens contain results, controls, short labels and actionable errors, not explanatory sentences. Put necessary explanations behind an explicit help action. Do not add paragraphs, repeated instructions, workflow narration or reassurance below controls or tables.
    • Preserve essential meaning in the main view: selected scope, units, the metric's basis and whether a result is approximate, partial or unavailable. Explanations may be disclosed on demand; material limitations must remain discoverable from a visible status beside the affected result.
    • Use compact, local indicators for routine partial coverage, excluded holdings and cached data. Reserve prominent warning banners for blocking problems or situations requiring timely action. Match color and visual prominence to the importance of the state; ordinary analytical limitations should not dominate the page.
    • Put secondary figures near their primary metric. Consolidate repeated status messages and avoid stacks of captions above or below a result. Keep controls aligned with the section they affect and use space to communicate hierarchy.
    • Preserve established styling and shared components. Improve density, placement and consistency before adding decorative elements, new panels or new navigation.
    • Import review must identify the affected statement, show the specific unresolved source rows locally, and offer a decision for that statement. Do not ask users to certify a batch with a blanket checkbox or infer a problem from an absent tax/fee row. Routine fully reconciled statements should not need manual approval.
    • Keep help and status details accessible by keyboard and touch, with meaningful labels. Color and hover alone must not carry essential information.

    Apply these principles across the application, including new features and error states. They govern presentation without weakening calculation or coverage rules.

    Apply chart_hover at each Plotly rendering boundary. Curves share a vertical date/value guide and formatted tooltips across the plot area; category and allocation charts retain their meaningful category tooltips. Preserve explicit units, missing values and purpose-built hover content. Pointer movement must not trigger Streamlit reruns. Overview history's value/capital boxes follow the hovered observations and include their dates. The inline gain switches from selected-period gain to total known gain during hover. Any range covering the full holding history uses the shared total known gain calculation, just like All. Personal and price histories use currency symbols consistently in summaries, axes and tooltips. Leaving restores the latest value and period gain. Clicking the gain badge toggles between amount and cumulative TWR; its metric name and period appear only in a concise tooltip. Return percentages use ↑/↓; monetary gains keep +/− signs. Zero and unavailable returns have no direction arrow. TWR defaults to the documented daily flow convention; no remaining-cost denominator is substituted. Exact event TWR remains explicit. Annualized money-weighted return always uses full recorded history, independently of the chart range or pointer. Keep one summary row below the view tabs. The allocation measure switch belongs above the allocation table, aligned with its left edge and the top of the chart. Performance keeps category comparison visible and leaves detailed category and position lists to their existing views.

    View changes and progressive disclosure

    Related views must share card widths, minimum heights and alignment. Keep their navigation and outer container stable. Position detail dialogs keep a viewport-bounded height across views and loading states; long content scrolls inside the window. Test the outer bounds throughout transitions, not just card dimensions. Replace the outgoing content before doing expensive work for the next view. Do not calculate a complete history merely to choose an already-selected tab, or repeat calculations during the same render. Reuse chart results only while journal facts, observation revisions, scope, currency, period and calendar date remain unchanged. Within and across chart ranges, share dated ledger states and previously calculated hover results without relaxing coverage checks. Keep this context session-local and expire it when workspace, journal, observations, reporting currency or calendar date changes. Fixed position headlines request only full-history summary results; they do not generate chart points. The Overview history chart, current-gain summary and category comparison share one calculation context for the same journal and observation snapshot. Current quotes remain separate from dated historical valuations. Recorded cost and cash components may be reused between transaction/fee dates; daily prices and reporting FX still resolve at the requested date. A new observation revision creates a new context, including when it arrives in a background fragment. Account history derives securities cash postings once per journal snapshot and reuses account states between cash/account event dates. A settlement value date alone does not release a pending receipt; explicit receipt events remain authoritative. Background data refreshes must remain local to the affected result. Dialog polling fragments must stop writing when their captured position or view is no longer selected; a stale timer must not draw into a replacement view. Verify actual transitions with delayed synthetic data: settled screenshots alone miss old controls and charts briefly appearing during a switch. An explicit retry rebuilds the owning dialog's download state and polling timer; successful completion must update the chart without changing its period or leaving the view. Idle polling checks the observation-file identity before parsing it again, while externally saved corrections still invalidate results.

    Position opening and clean dismissal rerun only the dialog host. Creating or discarding an unsaved editor draft refreshes the parent toolbar once so its resume action matches the retained draft. Child workflows share a local refresh boundary, including evidence saves, reconciliation, market evidence repairs and balance review. Persisted changes refresh current position values locally and refresh the parent page once after dismissal. Pending evidence alone does not repeat portfolio valuation. Streamlit 1.65 or newer is required for the keyed host callbacks; the runtime adapter is isolated in dialog_runtime.py and covered by browser lifecycle tests. Transaction list, import, edit and reconciliation screens use distinct keyed containers so their action rows are replaced together. Never leave list actions alongside review/apply controls. Evidence saves retain revision guards and leave balances unchanged until explicitly applied. Position detail tabs always use History, Key metrics, Transactions and Details. Without recorded history evidence, History shows the price chart directly. Otherwise it contains a Holdings / Price selector, defaulting to Holdings when useful holdings history exists and Price otherwise. Remember an explicit choice per position for the session, separately from the editor draft; clear these preferences when switching workspaces. Switching history type keeps the dialog and selector stable and replaces only the result beneath them. Position details keep one shared summary row above the chart selector: Position value with supported personal gain, Invested capital, and Annualized return. These figures retain their current/all-history basis across tabs, chart types, periods and pointer movement. Dated value, capital, total gain and TWR remain in chart tooltips; position charts do not rewrite headline cards. Return on cost and unrealized gain are listed in Details. Without applied history, capital uses the recorded acquisition cost and gain is explicitly unrealized only; annualized return remains unavailable. Applied history supplies the authoritative balance; missing evidence limits only the metrics that depend on it.

    Missing-data interfaces must identify the affected result and provide a relevant action. Do not expose a combined list of every metric's requirements as mandatory setup. Keep ordinary gaps subtle. Retrieve supported market data automatically; manual repair forms should preselect the position, date and currencies when known. Internal identities and fingerprints are storage details, not user-facing labels. Never display transaction references, transaction IDs or hash-based IDs in normal product UI: tables, selectors, labels, tooltips, notifications and errors must use readable names and transaction facts instead. If internal identifiers are needed for debugging, expose them only inside an explicitly opened internal diagnostics view, never in the normal import or transaction-review workflow. Show overwrite confirmation only when a value will actually be replaced.

    For a single holding, use Apply history after reconciliation. For multiple holdings, review each with Accept this holding, then Apply reviewed holdings in one atomic save. Changes to reviewed facts reset these balance-review decisions. Applying makes the selected ledger authoritative for the resulting holdings, including when it replaces a different current balance. There is no separate history-completeness confirmation, expiry date, or incomplete-apply mode. Return CSV imports to Positions without opening an arbitrary holding. Missing amounts, prices, FX, starting balances and unresolved transactions remain metric-specific limitations. Do not infer evidence before a starting snapshot or claim that applied records prove the absence of omitted real-world transactions. Legacy completeness records remain readable but do not gate results. Market-data correction stays separate from transaction reconciliation.

    Architecture and verification

    Business logic is independent of Streamlit:

    holdings → reporting-currency valuation → normalized exposures → optional ETF expansion → classifications
         → hierarchical aggregation → charts and tables

    Position filters run after valuation and before exposure normalization. The exposure table retains asset IDs and source-position metadata through ETF expansion. Provider parsing and refresh code are separate from the generic ETF transformation and aggregation engine. All chart types share the same aggregation engine. exposure_analysis.py owns source selection and normalized ETF expansion without Streamlit; the UI supplies selections and renders the resulting tables. currencies.py shares quote-subunit normalization across spot and historical prices.

    application.py selects price/FX services, checks leverage eligibility and prepares valuation diagnostics without Streamlit. input_cache.py returns named PortfolioInputs and copies cached inputs before handing them to calculations. ui.py owns workspace recovery, widget interactions, explicit refresh requests and lazy view dispatch. Optional fundamentals and risk remain in analytics_service.py; opening an unrelated view must not fetch them.

    Actual accounts join the same pipeline at the reporting-currency valuation boundary. account_journal.py replays native Decimal cash, explicit loans, futures lots and pending claims. actual_portfolio.py projects these into valued rows, replacing adopted cash holdings and retaining position/account provenance. portfolio_exposure.py then uses separate capital and signed-exposure measures; provider parsing and journal persistence do not belong in the aggregation engine.

    monitoring.py owns indicator and account-rule calculations; monitoring_market.py normalizes provider observations, monitoring_service.py schedules checks while a session is open, and monitoring_store.py persists rule revisions and alert episodes under the workspace lock. Monitoring does not change holdings or orders. tour_monitoring.py creates its example only after verifying the active tour-owned synthetic directory; it reuses the normal alert explanation and acknowledgment UI.

    Calculation contracts

    Account history advances the canonical replay engine through dated checkpoints, retaining at most 128 in its local timeline. Movement prefixes share an append-only log without changing earlier states. A dated query and complete-journal validation remain distinct: final adoption checks run only when requesting the complete state, and validation failures remain failures on retry. Explicit zero inception uses a validated zero-opening source marker; snapshots never imply unknown prehistory was empty.

    Long XIRR schedules can certify uniqueness by enclosing a sign-changing root and all proper discounted cash-flow prefixes with directed decimal intervals. A uniform prefix sign implies the quotient after removing that root has uniformly signed coefficients, hence no other positive roots. Otherwise the solver isolates stationary points after exponential differentiation at a coefficient sign boundary. Work and depth limits produce a separate computational diagnostic; accepted rates still require residual and conditioning checks against the original full schedule.

    CSV and PDF readers normalize evidence into the ordinary securities history. Transaction commands, corrections, removals, reconciliation and calculation eligibility depend on economic facts, never the entry source. Linked exchanges, transfers and asset fees are reviewed and applied together. Source references and replacement chains remain immutable provenance; identical reimports cannot undo a later edit or removal. Similar cross-source trades require explicit review.

    The small public CSV uses dates and stable references. File order supplies the initial same-day order, which can be overridden with order and edited in the normal transaction form. No execution timestamp is invented. Legacy CSV v1/v2 receipts and journal v1–v4 remain readable. Journal v5 supports independently identified corrections of imported events and transaction estimate qualifiers; simple CSV receipts store sparse normalized facts. Loading older records does not rewrite them or invalidate existing review fingerprints.

    New-position identities are staged until application. history_publication.py protects the holdings/journal pair with a durable private recovery intent and revision guards under the workspace lock. Interrupted publications roll back before the next workspace access; unexpected third-party edits stop recovery. Evidence-only saves leave portfolio balances unchanged.

    There is one history calculation path: forward holdings, daily TWR and annualized XIRR. Supplied estimates qualify dependent results rather than blocking them. Unknown amounts, missing prices/FX and incomplete transaction coverage remain separate limitations. Acquisition-cost estimates alone do not qualify percentage returns that do not depend on those costs. Legacy asset-performance.json files are preserved as inert private artifacts and do not control views or calculations.

    • gain_accounting.py supplies shared current and historical known-gain results. Security-only and account-only cash flows cancel across their common boundary; account equity includes actual futures once, not their display allocation again. Result coverage and valuation provenance are separate from amounts. Historical acquisition capital and return-opening market value are distinct. Summary-only and snapshot fallbacks remain explicit, and missing return evidence never shortens All. Historical renderers consume calculated gains/TWR in customdata; browser code formats them but does not reconstruct financial calculations.

    • Saved positions retain their instrument ID and stable position ID. A contribution has an analytical asset ID plus its source position/instrument identity; company grouping must not overwrite the original security identity.

    • exposures.project_contributions accepts an explicitly aligned measure and copies source dimensions. Current value, target fractions and performance amounts can use the same transformation without pretending to be the same financial measure. Reserved calculation columns are rejected as custom CSV metadata. New calculated fields must extend that reservation.
    • constituents.py interprets normalized constituent facts. Each consumer owns its coverage policy: an equity fund's residual can be equity exposure while remaining unresolved company exposure. Futures proxy coverage also retains its stricter treatment of unidentified and non-equity weights.
    • Actual contract facts use derivative_types.py; account_journal.py owns signed lot arithmetic, FIFO reductions, settlements and native postings. Settled P&L is already cash; only unsettled P&L and pending claims add derivative wealth. Notional is exposure, never wealth. Security collateral is a quantity reservation against an existing holding, not another asset.
    • account_observations.py and derivative_market.py keep dated marks and free exact-contract observations separate from cash postings. account_margin.py evaluates entered requirements and haircuts without inferring broker rules. eurex_quotes.py isolates the anonymous public quote protocol and exact monthly contract validation. futures_quotes.py retains automatic observations and refresh status separately from manual marks and the journal; futures_valuation.py selects absolute prices and derives local freshness. Quote timestamps and retrieval timestamps must remain distinct. Settlement reference time orders valuations; it is not a fabricated quote/publication time. Daily cash eligibility uses Europe/Berlin business dates, independent of whether the archive changed since the last worker run. account_planning.py previews integer trades without changing account facts.
    • FuturePosition and future_projections.py retain the legacy EUR sandbox's funded-strategy arithmetic and named capital, exposure and risk adapters. Legacy simulation value must not enter actual account projections.
    • generalized_studies.py consumes copied scenario inputs, native price paths and explicit FX, margin, rate and transfer assumptions. Its results never write the account journal. study_market.py validates history identity, currency and return convention before a proxy can drive a reconstruction.
    • Missing amounts are not zero. Callers retain completeness information when projecting known contributions. prepare_market_analysis returns separate known-value chart nodes and completeness-preserving table nodes. Composition percentages use selected exposure; portfolio impact uses whole-portfolio equity.

    Preparation results own their output frames; frozen result containers do not make pandas frames deeply immutable. Treat inputs as borrowed, copy before mutation, and verify conservation, source provenance and input nonmutation when changing these boundaries. company_analysis.py prepares company grouping and coverage; renderers consume the prepared results.

    Session lifetimes

    state_lifecycle.py is the single catalog of feature-owned navigation, workspace, currency and tour policies. Add an ordinary widget input to its feature's declaration, with the correct mount boundary. Inputs may survive lazy unmounts and are restored once on remount so the browser receives the retained value. Remount bookkeeping is consumed only after view dispatch, preserving it across early-return dialogs. Buttons, upload handles and editor patches must not be reassigned as ordinary input state. Imports and persistent editors keep their specialized adapters. Registered ordinary import review controls also use the Positions/Import remount boundary so a delayed navigation request cannot leave their browser value at its default while the backend retains the reviewed choice. The holdings uploader discards stale widget handles on that return while retaining the draft file bytes; explicitly removing an uploaded file still clears its retained selection. Calculation fingerprints stay with the calculators that understand their assumptions.

    Currency changes clear monetary drafts and prior monetary plans while retaining scope and percentage preferences. Workspace changes clear owned drafts/results; workspace-keyed analytics preferences remain isolated by their keys. Tour capture copies only declared restorable state. Exercise real navigation as well as pure transition tests, including date controls and nested lazy views.

    Document writes retain their workspace locking, revision checks and backup policies. Commands validating multiple documents must hold the workspace lock from validation through publication. Cache and document writers deliberately retain different failure/recovery policies; sharing serialization mechanics must not erase those differences. atomic_file.staged_text only creates and cleans up a same-directory text stage, optionally flushing and syncing its content. Callers choose serialization, locks, backups and revision checks, then explicitly replace the destination. Document writers sync before backing up and rechecking revisions; cache writers preserve their own validation and failure fallback. ETF snapshot publication still writes the manifest last; it is a separate multi-file protocol.

    money.py formats scalar amounts with explicit currency and no Streamlit state. currency_display.py supplies session reporting units and numeric formats for native grids. Actual account entries retain EUR/USD/GBP units; projections perform explicit conversion before display. Only legacy fixed-EUR sandbox views use money.euros; formatting never converts amounts. Missing values remain missing. Shared list columns retain numeric sorting and their browser formatter. charts.chart_config provides compact allocation controls and explicit zoom/pan/reset controls for scenario studies; each chart receives independent configuration containers.

    Analytics development notes

    These implementation notes are also published with the guide. See the task guides for application usage and formulas, and contributor instructions for privacy and development requirements. These notes contain no working portfolio data.

    Current product state

    • Navigation follows the UI refactor: Overview, Exposure, Positions, Rebalance.
    • Overview → Analytics shares the existing category selection, including its descendants. Accounts can be narrowed in Options. Exposure filters and position search do not change this scope.
    • Historical risk comes first. Calculate risk loads the estimates; subsequent refreshes are explicit. Benchmark and history window are in Options.
    • Risk shows beta, annualized volatility, benchmark correlation, per-holding beta and volatility contributions, and data coverage. The expandable correlation matrix uses display names without appended identifiers, a purple–teal diverging scale fixed at −1 to +1, a nearby color bar, and a larger responsive plot.
    • Valuation and income follow risk. Direct-stock P/E excludes ETFs and nonpositive ratios. Fund fees and cash distribution yields do include eligible ETFs. Concentration and underlying company exposure are separate views of risk.
    • Positions has Holdings, Valuation, Income & fees, and Risk views. Optional columns and settings live in Options. Selecting a row opens its existing details dialog; Key metrics contains fundamentals, definitions, provenance, and a private fund-fee editor. Holdings does not request analytics data.
    • Preserve the refactor's shared table interactions, short names, search, sort, keyboard access, row details, and separate pencil editing when extending views.

    Calculation contracts

    • Snapshot weights use current reporting-currency market values and combine repeated instrument IDs across accounts. Buy-in performance belongs to Overview → Performance.
    • P/E is the inverse of value-weighted earnings yield among covered profitable direct equities. It is not an arithmetic average, and fund-reported P/E is not blended into it. Coverage is essential to interpreting this subset statistic.
    • Fees are current fund values × annual fee rates. They are already reflected in prices and must not be deducted from gains a second time. Public fee metadata is matched by exact share class, not an ETF look-through proxy.
    • Cash yield is a trailing distribution estimate, not a forecast or actual dividends received. Verified accumulating share classes have zero cash yield.
    • Risk uses adjusted market-price histories, converted with historical FX to the reporting currency. The latest available price within each completed Friday-ending week is used. Empty weeks are not filled, and returns do not bridge missing endpoints.
    • Default benchmark is the direct MSCI ACWI net-return index (892400, NETR), requested in reporting currency and validated against returned metadata. Usable cached index levels precede a disclosed IUSQ.DE fallback. Custom tickers do not substitute automatically; neither index nor equity histories are stitched. Windows are 1, 3, or 5 years, default 3. All included holdings and the benchmark share the same sample of at least 52 weekly returns. Provider-supplied betas are not used.
    • Portfolio weekly return is the sum of holding weekly returns × today's weights. Beta is covariance with the benchmark / benchmark variance. Annualized covariance is weekly covariance × 52. Contributions to volatility sum to portfolio volatility; negative contributions are possible.
    • This is a hypothetical constant-weight allocation, not the user's historical return series. It requires no transaction history and does not use buy-in prices.
    • Cash in the reporting currency has zero FX returns. In ordinary asset analysis, manual/unlisted positions without supported history are excluded, covered weights are renormalized, and partial coverage is shown. Missing valuations prevent claiming whole-portfolio coverage. Integrated account leverage shows a labelled covered-holdings estimate with exclusions and fixed whole-portfolio net-equity weights; missing histories do not suppress otherwise usable metrics. Missing/nonpositive net equity or an insufficient common sample still prevents an estimate. Reviewed same-share equity history fallbacks are currency-converted independently and disclosed; valuation tickers and futures proxies stay separate.
    • ETFs use their own adjusted price histories for risk, not constituent expansion. Category/account subsets show standalone beta and volatility on selected capital, with portfolio beta contribution secondary. Ordinary subsets normalize covered net funded values; derivative subsets require positive, fully valued selected backing capital. Count unsettled claims once before adding risk-only FX rows; never use margin or bare-contract gains as capital. A missing subset base does not suppress an otherwise supported portfolio beta contribution. The benchmark remains selected even for non-equity categories. Subset and contribution metrics use the same sample, which can differ across categories.
    • Ordinary holdings use asset-normalized beta. Version 0.2.0 also supports actual-account leverage in EUR/USD/GBP, with a separate net-equity denominator. Asset-normalized beta must not be presented as levered equity beta; legacy sandbox risk remains EUR-only.
    • Actual futures risk uses signed notional with compatible contract-currency histories and converts native changes at dated FX. It must not apply the FX return on the whole futures notional. Foreign cash, claims and debt carry their own FX risk. A documented leveraged ETF factor changes current exposure; risk still uses that fund's own price returns without multiplying leverage twice.

    Potential extensions and decisions to make first

    These are ideas, not promises or already implemented features.

    1. Compare categories side by side. Show beta, volatility, value share, and coverage for sibling categories. Decide whether all categories must share a single common sample; independently estimated betas are less comparable. Distinguish category beta from its contribution to whole-portfolio beta.
    2. Current versus proposed allocation. Preview hypothetical target weights with beta, volatility, concentration, and coverage. Reuse a single historical sample and covariance matrix for both scenarios. Decide how this connects to Rebalance and how infeasible targets or missing histories are displayed.
    3. Beta stability. Add rolling beta or comparisons across windows, confidence intervals, and benchmark explanatory power. A point estimate alone should not imply a stable forecast. Do not confuse beta with total volatility.
    4. Joint funded-asset and futures planning. The app already provides actual-account futures plans alongside the capital planner. Joint routing between funded holdings and derivatives would need explicit funding, transfer and financing assumptions. A leveraged ETF's advertised daily multiple is neither a guaranteed multiyear return multiple nor its beta against an arbitrary benchmark.
    5. Downside scenarios. Separate simple beta-based shocks from historical constant-weight scenarios. Neither reconstructs personal performance or sets a worst-case loss bound. Define treatment of missing assets and costs first.
    6. Fund valuation coverage. Keep fund-reported P/E separate for now. Combining direct stocks and look-through fund earnings requires compatible earnings, dates, loss treatment, and coverage; do not silently average unlike ratios.
    7. Metric loading UX. Risk currently needs Calculate risk; fundamentals load when Analytics is selected. Decide whether to load risk automatically, retain results across scope changes, or add progress/background loading. Preserve clear freshness and partial-data reporting and avoid repeated network requests.
    8. Benchmark presets and persistence. Currently a ticker field and a shared benchmark per analytics view. Category-specific defaults, saved preferences, or named presets would require explicit scope and persistence choices.
    9. Large heatmaps. Consider top-N selection, ordering/clustering, and duplicate display-name disambiguation without reintroducing noisy ticker suffixes. Numeric axis coordinates already preserve distinct cells for duplicate names.

    Browser acceptance

    Use synthetic workspaces and preserve observable value, persistence and lifecycle assertions.
